# ADR-0009: The Golden Dataset Is Two Tiers, and the Public One Is Sufficient

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-08-20
- **Resolves**: `TODO(GOLDEN_DATASET_LICENSING)` (Milestone 6)
- **Refines**: ADR-0008's closing consequence — the golden set carries every live `name@version`
- **Principles engaged**: III (Deterministic Core), VIII (Reproducibility and Versioning), IX
  (Evaluation Is a Product Feature), XI (MVP Discipline), XII (Open-Source Quality)

## Context

Principle IX requires a golden dataset and regression evaluation over it, and rejects vague AI
quality claims as evidence in any plan or PR. Principle XII requires the suite to run for every
contributor. A public repository cannot ship real customer invoices. Those three facts do not fit
together on their own, and the constitution left the reconciliation open rather than letting it be
settled by whichever dataset landed first.

Each of the three obvious single-tier answers fails in a different place:

- **Vendor real documents.** The repository becomes undistributable — the documents are not ours to
  license onward, and invoices carry personal data. ADR-0007 put the project under Apache-2.0; a
  corpus we cannot grant rights to makes that grant a lie about part of the tree.
- **Reference a private corpus only.** Contributors can read metrics but cannot produce them.
  Evaluation degrades from a product feature into an internal report, which is the thing Principle
  IX exists to forbid. The second-order effect is worse than the first: a golden-set number quoted
  in a PR becomes unverifiable by the reviewer, so the quality gate rests on trust — the precise
  situation "vague AI quality claims are not acceptable evidence" was written against.
- **Synthetic documents only.** Safe, reproducible, free, and measuring a world where the documents
  were generated by the same people who wrote the extractor. It cannot exercise scan noise, OCR
  error, unusual layout, or the mixed-quality reality the ingest layer of Milestone 2 exists to
  handle. The arithmetic is real; the documents are not.

The tension is not between safety and realism. It is that **one dataset is being asked to serve two
audiences with different rights** — a contributor who must be able to run everything, and a
maintainer who needs to measure documents nobody may redistribute.

## Decision

Two tiers, deliberately distinct, and never averaged together.

| Tier | Lives in the repository | Who can evaluate it | What is in it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Yes, vendored | Anyone with a checkout, no credentials, no network | Synthetic documents from generators committed to the repository, plus permissively licensed or public-domain documents |
| **Restricted** | No — referenced by content hash only | Whoever holds the corpus | Documents docdoc may measure against but may not redistribute |

The load-bearing clause is the one that is easy to skip: **the public tier alone MUST be sufficient
to produce a complete, non-partial report.** A dataset whose public half is a demo, with the real
measurement happening privately, satisfies the letter of this ADR and none of its purpose.

### What each tier obliges

- A run without the restricted tier produces a report **marked partial**, naming what it skipped and
  stating the covered fraction. It does not produce a smaller full report. A metric computed over an
  unannounced subset is the failure this whole structure exists to prevent: it reports success over
  whichever documents happened to be available, which are usually the easy ones.
- **Every** document in either tier records its origin and the basis on which docdoc may use it. A
  document whose provenance cannot be stated is not admitted. This applies to the public tier too —
  "found on the internet" is not a basis.
- A **synthetic** document records the generator and the generator version that produced it, so a
  label is traceable to the thing that made the document it describes.
- Disclosure is a **property of the tier, not of the caller's diligence.** Under a restricted tier,
  the report carries hashes in place of document text and field values and states that it did so.
  Nothing relies on a maintainer remembering to redact.
- Document text, field values, and label values never appear in log output, in either tier.

### Predictions follow the same split

A recorded prediction carries the values it extracted, so a prediction over a restricted document is
restricted for exactly the reason the document is.

- **Public tier: recorded, committed, replayed.** Scoring needs nothing but a checkout — no
  credentials, no provider, no network, no spend.
- **Restricted tier: produced by an opt-in recording step**, never committed.

Recording is **not** part of evaluating. The recording step is the only place in the evaluation layer
where a provider, credentials, or a network are used; the scorer only ever reads recorded artifacts.
That boundary is what keeps evaluation inside the deterministic core of Principle III while the
thing it measures stays probabilistic — and it is what makes byte-identical reports achievable at
all.

### Schema coverage

ADR-0008 allows concurrent majors and closes by noting that evaluation reports metrics per
`name@version` and never aggregated per name. The golden set therefore carries labels for every live
version it claims to cover, and a label written under `invoice@1` says nothing about a result
produced under `invoice@2`. Scoring across a differing schema identity or hash is refused rather
than approximated.

### The target size for quality gate 5

Gate 5 makes the evaluation gate advisory "until the golden dataset reaches its target size" and
never said what that size was, which left the gate unable to flip on any stated ground. It is:

**50 documents and 500 labeled fields, in the public tier, across at least two schemas, with at
least twenty documents per schema.**

Two things fix that shape:

- **Counted on the public tier alone**, because gate 5 is a CI gate and CI cannot see the restricted
  tier. A gate that flips on data the gate cannot read is not a gate.
- **500 labeled fields** puts one field outcome at roughly 0.2% of headline accuracy. This is a
  judgement about resolution, not a power calculation: below it, ordinary movement is
  indistinguishable from the regressions a blocking gate exists to stop, and a gate that fires on
  noise gets disabled by the third contributor it inconveniences.

Reaching the target does not flip the gate. Flipping it is a further constitution amendment, on
evidence that the metrics have been stable enough to block a merge on.

## Consequences

- **Two tiers is more machinery than one**, and Principle XI's default is the simpler option. Taken
  anyway because the simpler options each break something the constitution requires outright, and
  because the partial-report and disclosure rules that carry most of the cost are demanded by
  Principle IX's honesty requirements whether or not a second tier exists.
- **The public tier's numbers are the ones anyone can check, so they are the ones that will be
  quoted.** Early on that tier will be synthetic-heavy, and synthetic documents are easier than real
  ones, so headline accuracy will flatter. Mitigated but not solved by reporting per tier and never
  merging them, and by the manifest declaring composition. Worth stating plainly: an early public
  accuracy figure is a floor on difficulty, not an estimate of field performance.
- **Maintainers carry a dataset CI never sees.** It can rot — labels drifting against a corpus no
  test exercises. The hash reference catches a changed document; it catches nothing about a stale
  label. Accepted as the price of measuring real documents at all.
- **Somebody has to write the generators**, and a generator that only emits documents the parser
  already handles measures the parser's existing competence. Generator variety is real work and does
  not arrive with this ADR.
- **The restricted tier can never make the gate blocking**, by construction. If real-document
  measurement is ever to gate merges, that needs a different mechanism than gate 5 — recorded here
  so it is not discovered late.
- **A contributor can produce a complete evaluation for free**, which is the property that makes the
  metrics in a PR checkable by the reviewer rather than assertions by the author. That is the whole
  point, and it is the one consequence the other five are paying for.
